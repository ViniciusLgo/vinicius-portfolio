Sometimes I catch myself trying to learn too many things at the same time.

One more technology, one more course, one more project idea.

But not everything needs to happen right now. Not everything I learn needs to turn into a system, a repository, or a publication. Some things just exist to broaden my view and prepare me better for the next problem.

## What I learned about focus

I've been trying to pick one priority, strengthen the foundation, and leave the rest for later.

Because focus is also about knowing what **not** to do right now.

It's easy to get lost in the sheer amount of content out there. There's always a new language, a new framework, a new paradigm promising to be the next big leap. But real growth happens when you go deeper into what you already know, not when you just keep piling up things you haven't mastered yet.

## In practice

When I notice I'm jumping from topic to topic without finishing anything, I stop and make a simple list:

- What do I need to learn right now for my current job?
- What do I want to learn over the next 6 months?
- What can wait?

That separation helps me stay focused without giving up on curiosity.
